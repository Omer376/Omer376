<summary><b>Airbnb Market Analysis</b> · 280K listings cleaned across 10 cities</summary>

<br/>

<img src="https://omer376.github.io/proj8-screenshot-1.png" width="100%" alt="Airbnb Market Analysis dashboard" />

| Timeline | Dataset | Tools | Role |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Sep 2026 | 280K listings · 10 cities | MySQL, Power BI, DAX | Solo Analyst |

**Problem.** With an overall satisfaction score of 62.88/100, Airbnb needed to know what actually drives guest ratings and how pricing, room type, and amenities affect them. The raw listings data was messy, with nulls, blanks, and inconsistent host-location entries.

**Approach.** Loaded the listings into MySQL, took a backup table, removed the 4 rows with null values, and confirmed there were no duplicate listing IDs. Converted blanks to proper nulls or "Not provided", standardised 180+ messy host-location values (country codes, city names, junk text) into country names, dropped unused columns, and engineered an `amenity_count` column. Modelled the cleaned data in Power BI with DAX measures, a Key Influencers visual, a decomposition tree, and price-bucket analysis.

<img src="https://omer376.github.io/proj8-screenshot-3.png" width="100%" alt="MySQL data cleaning script for Airbnb listings" />

**Impact**
- **280K listings cleaned** and made analysis-ready, with an average price of $150/day and about 20 amenities per listing.
- **Rating drivers:** communication (+18.3) and cleanliness (+18.14) are the biggest drivers of guest rating; location matters far less (+8.19).
- **Cheaper listings score better:** the $50–100 tier averages 68.45, while listings over $1,000 drop to 52.91.
- **Market structure:** entire-place listings make up about 65% of the market (182K) and rate highest (68.08); shared rooms rate lowest (41.74).
- **Priciest cities:** Bangkok ($1,100) and Cape Town ($1,069) average more than New York or Paris, and ratings rise sharply up to about 20 amenities before flattening.

<summary><b>Dubai Real Estate: Executive Summary</b> · $71B+ market activity analyzed</summary>

<br/>

<img src="https://omer376.github.io/proj7-screenshot-1.png" width="100%" alt="Dubai Real Estate Executive Summary dashboard" />

| Timeline | Dataset | Tools | Role |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Aug 2026 | 5 relational tables · 93,439 rows · Jan 2020 – Apr 2026 | Power BI, DAX, Power Query, Excel | Solo Analyst |

**Problem.** Investors had no consolidated view connecting market-wide pricing trends, rental yield performance, and developer-level activity, making it hard to decide where to allocate capital across $71bn+ in tracked activity.

**Approach.** Built a star schema around two shared dimensions (Community and Date) feeding four fact tables (off-plan, secondary sales, rentals, monthly area prices), with a metro stations table for proximity data and a feature-engineering layer for measures like average annual rent. Cleaned and validated all 93,439 rows in Excel and Power Query before modelling.

**Impact**
- **$71.08bn in total market sales** consolidated into one view, with an 8.25% average gross rental yield and about $84K average annual rental income.
- **Yield sweet spot:** mid-tier properties under $1,000/sq.ft yield 8–12%, while premium properties near $2,000/sq.ft yield closer to 7%.
- **Market concentration:** Emaar holds a 74.5% developer share at $1.2K/sq.ft, roughly 30% above the next tier of developers.
- **Recommendation:** investors prioritising yield over prestige should target mid-priced secondary properties in high-volume communities rather than premium off-plan developments.

<summary><b>Celestial Airline Service Satisfaction</b> · 57%+ dissatisfaction diagnosed</summary>

<br/>

<img src="https://omer376.github.io/proj6-screenshot.png" width="100%" alt="Celestial Airline Service Satisfaction dashboard" />

| Timeline | Dataset | Tools | Role |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Jul 2026 | Kaggle Airline Satisfaction · 129,881 rows × 24 columns | Power BI, Power Query, Excel | Solo Analyst |

**Problem.** The airline was fielding a wave of service complaints and needed to know whether flight service quality was driving overall satisfaction, and which service factors were the real bottlenecks.

**Approach.** The dataset arrived as a single wide table with 14 service-rating columns, so a flat schema was used rather than forcing a star model that didn't fit. Cleaned and validated all 129,881 rows in Power Query, standardised rating fields, and built calculated fields in Power BI to break satisfaction down by service category, delay time, distance, and traveller type.

**Impact**
- **Overall satisfaction sat at 3.24/5**, dropping to 2.95 among the ~57% of customers who reported being dissatisfied.
- **Weak spot:** online services rated only 2.91/5 vs 3.42/5 for on-board services; recommended improving online booking and Wi-Fi first.
- **Delay effect:** satisfaction dropped as delays increased (average delay ~15 min), while longer flights correlated with higher satisfaction.
- **Target:** lift every service rating to 3.6+ to shift the satisfied-customer share, plus a loyalty program for the largely returning, older, business-travel customer base.

<summary><b>Global Electronics Retailer Sales Report</b> · 5-table retail dataset, 2016–2021</summary>

<br/>

<img src="https://omer376.github.io/proj5-screenshot.png" width="100%" alt="Global Electronics Retailer Sales Report dashboard" />

| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Jul 2016 – Jan 2021 | Maven Analytics · Customers, Products, Sales, Stores, Exchange Rates | Excel, Power BI | Solo Analyst |

**Problem.** Leadership needed to find inefficiencies, hidden profit drivers, and operational gaps across products, stores, and customers, including why high-revenue products weren't always the most profitable and why delivery complaints kept piling up.

**Approach.** Built a star-schema model in Power BI with Sales as the fact table, a dedicated date table for time intelligence, and exchange rates merged into Sales to standardise every figure in USD. **Impact**
- **Computers led revenue** at $19.3M with a 58% margin.
- **Hidden margin opportunity:** Music, Movies & Audio Books carried the highest margin (61%) despite the lowest revenue ($3.1M).
- **Data integrity flag:** every country showed an identical store count of 67, pointing to a likely broken relationship worth investigating.
- **Operations gap:** about 79% of deliveries were logged as missing, improving only 11 points since 2016, which led to a recommendation for deeper delivery-data analysis.
